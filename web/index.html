Evidence with limits

What the research shows, and where it stops.

Slow breathing reliably changes cardiovascular measures such as heart rate variability (HRV) while you practise. Studies also report possible benefits for stress, mood, and anxiety, but the results vary and lasting effects remain uncertain. No single pace or technique has been shown to be ideal for everyone.

That is how önd presents the evidence: what was studied, what changed, and where the limits are. Breathe comfortably rather than exactly to the count. If a pattern makes you dizzy or tingly, ease back or return to normal breathing.

Cyclic Sighing can support a calmer daily practice

Cyclic Sighing asks you to breathe in twice, then take one easy, longer breath out. In one month-long study, five minutes a day improved mood and resting breathing rate more than three comparison practices. The study was small and has not yet been repeated.

Heart and Health

See your heart rate during and after a session.

During a connected phone-and-Watch session, önd can show your live Watch heart rate on the phone. Afterwards, a simple curve shows how it moved. There is no target zone or readiness number, and a single session cannot tell you whether the practice worked.

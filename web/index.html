Evidence with limits

Useful research, without turning uncertainty into a promise.

Slow-paced breathing reliably changes cardiovascular measures such as heart-rate variability while you practise. Reported effects on stress, mood, and anxiety are promising, but they are smaller and less consistent across studies, and longer-term effects are less certain. No single pace or technique has been shown to be ideal for everyone.

That is how önd presents the evidence: what was studied, what changed, and where the limits are. A comfortable breath matters more than a perfect count. If a pattern makes you dizzy or tingly, ease back or return to normal breathing.

One pattern, carefully described

Cyclic sighing uses two inhales followed by an easy, longer exhale. It has been studied as a brief daily practice; that does not make one sigh an instant reset or the pattern universally superior.

Heart and Health

Context, never a score.

During a connected phone-and-Watch session, önd can show your live Watch heart rate on the phone. Afterwards, a simple curve shows how it moved. There is no target zone, readiness number, or verdict that the practice worked: one session is context, not a diagnosis.

Your practice in Apple Health
With permission, completed sessions write Mindful Minutes. Optional before-and-after check-ins write directly to State of Mind and are not stored by önd.
Trends you choose to see
önd+ can show weekly context from sleeping breathing rate, resting heart rate, and heart-rate variability. Access is off until you enable it, uses coarse averages, and never turns the readings into a health score.

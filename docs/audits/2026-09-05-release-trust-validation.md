## Catalogue claims

All 13 catalogue exercises were checked against the sources below. The rows map the retained empirical claims to their supporting studies and distinguish study protocols from önd's defaults. Preparation instructions and subjective descriptions are not clinical efficacy findings. This is a targeted substantiation review, not a systematic search of all published research.

| Exercise | Source and study scope | Supported claim and limit | Remediation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Box Breathing | [Balban 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/): remote randomized comparison of three breathing practices and mindfulness, five minutes daily for a month. | Mood improved in breathing groups; the clearest comparison with mindfulness was cyclic sighing. Equal counts and holds were not isolated as necessary ingredients. | Removed the unverified three-study 2026 summary and the claim of superiority inferred from comparisons between groups. |
| Coherent Breathing | [Fincham 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-49279-8): 400 adults, 5.5 versus 12 breaths/minute, ten minutes daily for four weeks. [Broader review](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-27247-y). | No clear advantage over the credible breathing control. Broader breathwork trials report modest average psychological benefits; these are not unique to this pace. | Explicitly states that the trial used longer sessions than the app default. Removed an unlinked personal-resonance trial claim. |
| 4-7-8 Breathing | [Kirazli 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12895279/): 48 people with tinnitus; six-week practice plus information versus information alone. | Self-reported sleep and psychological measures improved. Clinical population and lack of a matched breathing control limit generalization. | Replaced the weakly sourced 15-study summary and absolute claim that no study has ever found superiority. No healthy-sleeper or exact-count superiority claim. |
| Extended Exhale | [Czub 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39476253/): 828 participants, one ten-minute session, varied pace and ratio. [Birdee 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36871835/): 100 adults, 12 weeks, longer exhale versus equal counts. | Slower rate was associated with lower post-session anxiety in Czub. Birdee found no clear between-group stress advantage for extending the exhale. | Removed the inference that Birdee independently established that pace caused the benefit. Both sources now appear in the app. |
| Physiological Sigh | [Balban 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/): five-minute daily sighing, not two cycles. | The cited trial does not establish the effect of a two-sigh reset. | Removed the rapid-reset efficacy promise, unverified short-dose pilot summary and alveolar/CO₂ explanation presented as proof of emotional benefit. |
| Cyclic Sighing | [Balban 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/): daily practice over one month. | Improved mood and lower respiratory rate; comparison supports caution about claims of superiority over every breathing alternative. | Removed the all-three-comparators superiority claim and the absolute non-replication claim. Discloses that the trial used a mouth exhale while önd defaults to nasal exhalation and fixed timings. |
| Pursed-Lip Breathing | [2024 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39477355/): 73 randomized trials of multiple breathing methods in serious respiratory illness, mostly COPD/asthma. | The pursed-lip/diaphragmatic subgroup's average breathlessness change was below the review's clinical importance threshold. The entire 73-trial set was not exclusively pursed-lip breathing. | Corrected the study scope and removed the unsupported inference about what most individual patients would notice. No healthy-person calm benefit is established. |
| Humming Breath | [Weitzberg and Lundberg 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12119224/): small physiological experiment in healthy participants. | Humming increased measured nasal nitric oxide. It did not measure improved mood, sleep or treatment of sinus infection. | Removed broad unlinked emotional-benefit claims and the implication that the chemical measurement establishes health benefit. |
| Cooling Breath | [2020 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32379673/): 100 people with hypertension, three months of Sheetali practice. | Blood pressure and HRV were studied. This does not establish whole-body cooling or a benefit in healthy users. | Added a specific native source. Replaced an absolute literature-absence claim with the actual study boundary. |
| Bellows Breath | [Telles 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30233116/): 25 healthy women, reaction-time testing across breathing and comparison conditions, 18-minute sessions. | A traditional protocol and a reaction-time measure do not validate the app's approximately 40-second energy/focus round. | Added the source and dose mismatch. Removed the unqualified energy/focus promise and assertion that longer practice adds no energy. |
| Wim Hof-style Rounds | [Fincham 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39043650/): 200 healthy young adults, 20 minutes daily for three weeks, fast breathing/long holds versus a gentler breathing control. | Stress improved in both groups; no clear between-group difference in stress or other measured mental-health outcomes. Inflammation was not an outcome. | Corrected “no change” to no clear between-group advantage. Removed the inflammation and greater-side-effects claims and an unlinked 84-person comparison. Does not validate the app's exact sequence. |
| Long Box Breathing | [Balban 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/) and the [broader review](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-27247-y). | Background evidence for breathing practice, not a trial of six-count sides or improved task performance. | Added a relevant source with an explicit extrapolation warning. Replaced universal “no trial” claims with the scope of the cited research. |
| Alternate-Nostril Breathing | [2024 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39008954/): blood-pressure meta-analysis with substantial heterogeneity. [Kamath 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5660749/): 30 students, 15-minute practice before simulated public speaking. | Pooled blood-pressure changes vary widely across trials. The pilot found no clear anxiety difference between groups. Neither establishes an added benefit from nostril switching or a focus benefit. | Added both native links and narrowed the claim about what studies have isolated. |

Slow-breathing physiological explanations use the distinction between immediate respiratory/heart-rate changes and lasting clinical benefit described in [Russo 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5709795/). No measured chemical or heart-rate change should be presented as proof of improved wellbeing.

## Safety and consent

The 13 Basics entries and 17 Moments were re-read alongside catalogue cautions and the coach instructions. Existing remediations retain permission to stop, avoid maximum-effort holds, direct new/persistent breathlessness to medical advice, and direct sudden/severe difficulty or associated chest pain to urgent help. The emergency boundary is consistent with [NHS breathlessness guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/shortness-of-breath/). This is a consistency check, not clinical approval of contraindications or protocols.

The following still need qualified review: fast breathing followed by open-ended holds; maximum adjustable timings; suitability and supervision of the child route; evidence-grade calibration; and the physiological leaderboards. Those rankings still reward slower breathing or longer holds despite neutral explanatory copy. Do not treat a source-backed paragraph as approval of that product choice.
